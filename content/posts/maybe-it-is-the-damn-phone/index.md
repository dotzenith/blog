# The Problem with the Phone

Perhaps [this has already been discussed extensively and I am just 24](https://x.com/Gnoc290438/status/2072115759384600939),
but interacting with the internet on the phone really sucks as of late.

It wasn't always like this, and not to muse about "good old days", but I do have fond memories of browsing the internet at
2G speeds on my old blackberry with a jammed scroll wheel. Things were broken of course, but the internet never felt hostile
as a mobile user, nowadays it does.

For example, I cannot open Reddit on my phone's browser without being blocked from reading the rest of the post
and being pushed to use the terrible Reddit mobile app
(rest in peace Apollo (I know Apollo reborn exists, I still use it (are we lisp yet?)))

Reddit is not alone in this, damn near every website on the internet detects that I'm on my phone, and
just wants me to use their terrible react-native expo app.

Since the browser on my phone is barely a real browser, and won't let me use the same extensions as I normally
would on the desktop, I'm just stuck like this (in a walled garden even).

# The Problem with the Phone only gets worse

The situation above is just one of my grievances with the phone.
Admittedly, a lot of this stems from being an iPhone user (the days of a rooted pixel are long gone for me), and maybe things
are better over in GrapeheneOS land. I just can't help but feel this deep sense of annoyance at being babied by my phone.

Phones are locked down by design, and we are told this is for the benefit of the user.
Despite those claims however, the average app on the phone has way more access to me and my device than the average website does on
the trendiest hardened firefox fork I can run on desktop.

Random social media app can tell you've taken a screenshot, random banking app can tell
your phone is jailbroken/rooted, and up until recently, random mobile game could farm oodles of information about you to sell
to advertisers (I mean they still can, it's just slightly harder).

# Maybe sideloading can save us?

So if trying to access the website on my phone's browser sucks, and the official app also sucks, maybe I can use a modded
client for some of these apps? Surely that will fix at least some of my problems right?

**Extremely loud incorrect buzzer noise**

Sideloading on iOS is still awful, and I keep hearing that google is trying to make it harder on Android as well.
Even if you manage to get past the hurdle of sideloading the modded client, good luck actually using it.

This is certainly the case with twitter (I'm not calling it by the other name), which actively blocks 3rd party clients now.
Reddit had this whole big thing about making API access basically unfeasible, which lead to
Apollo's (maybe the best 3rd party client for any social media ever?) death. For a while you could get around this by
getting your own API key, but Reddit has cracked down on that as well.

# What do?

So far, we've basically established that: shit sucks.

I believe the answer is to just use the phone less. Now, before I lose everyone's interest, I'm not saying we should
abandon the phone, that would also be annoying. I'm just saying, maybe we should just use the phone for texting, calling,
and the occasional food delivery order, while using the desktop/laptop for the internet.

On the desktop, you get a real browser, with real browser extensions, and real control (for the most part) over your device.
No one will bother you about their terrible app because they don't have a terrible app (or an app at all) for the desktop.

Take as many screenshots as you'd like, go to any banking site on your hardened firefox fork, and use uBlockOrigin everywhere.

# Better things are possible

Now, you might think that I am being paid by big desktop to sell more desktop, and I'm here to tell you: I wish.

Browsing the internet on the desktop can still suck sometimes.
Some sites will break on your hardened firefox fork, others will tell you to turn off your ad-blocker extension.

The good thing fortunately, is that a lot of these problems are temporary hurdles on the desktop.
Sites rarely break on firefox for me these days, and when they do, I open up chrome to deal with it real quick and then swiftly go back to the comfort of firefox.
uBlockOrigin keeps getting better at dodging ad-block detection, and when it doesn't, I turn it off for moment, do my business on whatever site it is that I'm on, and then move on with my day.

The point being, that a lot of these problems are not problems of the platform itself, and more often than not, there is a workaround.

# This is the year of the linux desktop?

Unfortunately, this is not the year of the linux desktop, but it could be the year of The **Desktop**, or The **Laptop**.

So, just it a shot. Do more of your old fashioned *web surfing* on the desktop, use your laptop in bed, and discover the joys of a platform that will (mostly) let you do whatever you want.
